Knowledge Base Assistant

Ask a question about any of the 450 Wikipedia articles in its knowledge base. It finds the right passage with hybrid search and a learned re-ranker, then quotes the answer word for word with a citation. When the knowledge base doesn't contain the answer, it says so instead of guessing.

How it works

Everything runs on CPU inside one serverless function

Keyword search (BM25) and embedding search each pull their top 50 passages. A small neural ranker, trained on 15,000 separate questions to put the right passage first, orders the merged list using 16 signals: both scores, how many of the question's rare words each passage contains, how tightly they cluster within one or two sentences, and title overlap.

A second model picks the sentence most likely to contain the answer from the top three passages, so every answer is a verbatim quote with a citation. It can't invent text. A confidence model, trained on questions about articles deliberately left out of the index, decides when to decline. On this dataset keyword search beats embeddings by a wide margin, and naively blending them made results worse. The re-ranker is what makes the combination pay off.
